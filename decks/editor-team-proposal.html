Proposal

A small editing team,
instead of rehiring one every event.

A small editing team working alongside me. I would handle the sourcing, the training and the standards, and keep supporting Crystal as I do now.

The problem

It is not a workload problem.
It is a knowledge problem.

We rotate through different editors event to event. Every one of them starts from zero: no understanding of our events, our editing style, or what we count as a strong reference. All of it gets re-explained every time.

Rotating editors

Back to zero every time.

A standing team

Each event starts where the last one finished.

What that costs us

  • Output slows while people are brought up to speed
  • Quality swings event to event
  • Revision rounds repeat the same corrections
  • The learning leaves when the editor does

What a standing team changes

  • Understanding compounds instead of resetting
  • Second event is faster than the first
  • Corrections are made once, not every event
  • Quality becomes predictable

Approach

Local, and proven before we commit

Why a Filipino team

  • Cultural and communication alignment we already have
  • Shorter onboarding, less friction per brief
  • More cost-effective at the same standard

Why test first, either way

  • We should not commit to anyone — local or international — before seeing their output
  • A paid one-day trial shows more than any interview
  • The process below is built to find that out cheaply

The geography argument is secondary. The argument for testing before hiring holds regardless.

Hiring

Six steps, deliberately light

1

Source talent.

Referrals, portfolios, freelance platforms.
2

Sign the confidentiality agreement.

Before any paid work begins. Draft in the appendix.
3

First interview — Dioné and Ally.

Experience, availability, working style. Screening only.
4

Paid one-day trial.

A set of clips, minimal direction. How much they finish, and how they read the assets. This is the real signal.
5

Review and short report.

I assess the output and write it up for you.
6

Decide together.

Event partner or not. No second interview — the role is an event partnership, not a full-time hire.
+

Optional.

A secondary review with Crystal and Susan if useful. Skipped by default so candidates are not pushed through more process than the role warrants.

Later, selectively: anyone who shows a consistently strong eye across several events converts to full-time, with scope widening beyond events into regular social content.

Engagement shape

What the partnership looks like

Editors come in before the event, not on the day. That single change fixes most of what went wrong at the US Open — briefing and strategy happen while there is still time to act on them.

Smaller events2–3 days ahead
BriefStrategyPrepEvent day
Bigger events5–7 days ahead
BriefStrategyRefsPrepPrepCheckReadyEvent day

Two ways to price the lead-in — open to either, or a mix

Option A

Lower rate on briefing days

Full days, priced below the event-day rate. Simple to plan around, and they are available for the whole day if something moves.

Option B

Shorter briefing days

Same rate, but briefing runs as a half day rather than eight hours. Briefing rarely needs a full day anyway.

Initial thinking, not a fixed position. What matters is that the lead-in is planned and paid for, rather than squeezed into the night before.

What I build, and who we look for

The team only works if two things are true

What I build

  • Brand editing standards, written from scratch
  • Training on reading outliers — and sourcing them independently
  • Training on Nancy captions and on-screen text

Who we hire for

  • We move fast, and briefs get built on the fly
  • The requirement is not a flawless brief — it is people who work well inside an evolving one
  • Adaptive, not passive. Invested in improving, not just executing
  • Culture, adaptability and a genuinely good eye — scouted properly, not sourced out of scarcity once an event is already on us

Looking back

What hurt, and what we have already fixed

Before ASAP HK and the US Open
Then

The production team edited without hooks. Finding the hook and adding the captions came back to me afterwards — the wrong order, and it does not scale.

Now

We work backwards. The reference, or the music, comes before the edit.

ASAP HK
Then

Just Remo and me, and I wrote every caption and text overlay myself.

Now

The partnership got better as we went, and it became clear he could be trained to write them. A system to support that is in progress.

US Open
Then

We had editors but had not met them beforehand. Everything landed on the day, and the gaps surfaced there.

Now

The standards are written down, and the first proper feedback session with the editors has happened.

Each of these got better because we caught it and changed it. A standing team is how that stops being accidental.

Terms

What is fixed, and what we can shape

Nonnegotiable
  • Confidentiality on unreleased footage, event content and brand assets
  • Clear scope per engagement — cutting, captions, overlays and our standards in; filming and licensed music out
  • Ownership — final files and source footage are the company’s once paid
  • Responsiveness during live event hours
  • Firm deadline on event-day deliverables, same or next day
Open to discussion
  • Pay structure — hourly, day rate or per deliverable
  • Revision rounds before extra charges
  • Turnaround on evergreen content
  • Exclusivity between events
  • Conversion criteria and timeline
  • Equipment and software provided or self-supplied
  • Payment schedule
