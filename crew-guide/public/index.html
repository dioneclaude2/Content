The one that gets things killed

Editing first and looking for inspiration afterwards.

This can never happen. If the cut already exists and we go hunting for a reference for the caption or the text overlay, that overlay is a guess dressed up as a decision. It was not chosen for a reason, because at the moment it was chosen there was no reason available. Unintentional never performs — and you can feel it in the content, even when you cannot point at what is wrong.

01

What counts as an outlier

An outlier is not the post with the most views. It is the post that beat its own account's normal by the widest margin. That gap is the only thing that tells you the format did the work rather than the follower count.

Rule of thumb

Views ÷ that account's median

Pull up the creator's recent posts, eyeball the middle one, divide. 5× or better and you have an outlier. Under 2× is just a good day.

The gold

Small account, huge numbers

A 4k-follower account with 900k views is worth ten big-account posts. Nothing carried it but the idea and the execution — which means it is copyable.

Careful

Big account, big numbers

Usually just the audience showing up. Not an outlier, and not a lesson. Check it against their own median before you save it.

Outlier check

Do the maths before you save. Median = the middle-of-the-road view count on that creator's recent posts, not their best one.

02

How to hunt

Hunting is a scheduled job, not something you do when you are stuck. Blocks of it, before a project is planned — so there is always a full swipe file to build ideas out of.

Search the format, not the topic

Type the shape of the thing into search, not our product. get ready with me tennis, day in the life nyc, outfit transition. Topic search finds competitors; format search finds mechanics.

Sort by what performed, then filter by recency

A format that worked eighteen months ago is a period piece. Anything older than about three months, check that it is still running before you build on it.

Check the ratios, not just the views

Saves and shares beat likes. A high save rate means people wanted it later — that is the signal that the idea was strong rather than the face.

Read the comments for the reason

The comments will usually tell you exactly what landed — the outfit, the line, the twist at 4 seconds. That sentence is your "why" line when you save it.

Hunt outside our category

Most of what we can steal cleanly comes from outside fashion. Food, travel, gaming and sports edits solve hooks and pacing problems we have, and nobody will recognise the source.

Stop at enough, not at perfect

Ten to fifteen strong outliers is a planning session. Two hundred saved posts nobody annotated is a graveyard.

03

Save it properly

A saved post with no note is worthless in a week — you will remember that you liked it and not what for. Never save without the why line. It takes fifteen seconds and it is the entire value of the swipe file.

LINK paste the post SCORE views ÷ their median, e.g. 15x WHY IT WORKED one sentence, the mechanism — not "nice edit" STEAL the exact part we are taking: hook / overlay cadence / cut rhythm FOR which project or format this is a candidate for
Good why line

“Hook is a question on screen at 0:00 with the answer withheld until 0:06 — the pause is what holds people, not the outfit.”

Useless why line

“Love this — great vibes, cool transitions.” Nothing here can be rebuilt. In a week this is a screenshot of a stranger.

04

Copy & replicate — properly

Copying the mechanism is the job. Copying the subject is theft and it does not even work. Break the outlier into its parts, take the parts that made it move, swap everything else for ours.

HookFirst 1 second

What is on screen and what is being withheld. This is the single most copyable part of any outlier — and the part most people skip.

StructureThe shape

How many beats, what order, where the turn lands. Write it out as a list before you plan the shoot.

Text overlaysCadence & placement

How many words, how long they hold, where they sit, when they change. Decided at planning, never in the edit.

Cut rhythmPacing

Count the cuts in the first five seconds. That number is the format's pulse — match it rather than guessing.

AudioTrack & sound design

Whether the cut serves the track or the track sits under the cut. Note where location sound is doing the work.

EndingLast 2 seconds

Loop, payoff, or hard stop. The ending is what decides a rewatch, and rewatch is what decides reach.

Take

The mechanism

Hook shape, beat order, overlay cadence, cut rhythm, ending type. All of it is format, and format belongs to nobody.

Never take

The specifics

Their exact words, their joke, their song choice where it is the point, their product moment, their face. Copy those and you have made their content with our logo on it.

05

Our editing style

The house look. This is what a Nancy edit is supposed to feel like regardless of which project it came from.

Everything movesConfirm

Every setup has motion in it. If a shot sits still for more than a beat it is either a deliberate hold or it is out.

Shoot doublesConfirm

One clean digital pass, one lo-fi. Both passes live in the same cut — the contrast between them is the look.

Keep the blurry onesConfirm

Out-of-focus and lo-fi frames are selects, not mistakes. At least one survives into every deliverable.

Never colour-match the passesConfirm

Do not grade the lo-fi toward the digital to "unify" a sequence. That difference is the point of shooting twice.

Cut on movementConfirm

Cut on motion, not on the beat grid. Wide → detail → face is the order that reads as intent.

Location sound staysConfirm

Real sound under the music. It is what stops a cut reading like a stock edit. No effects for things that were not there.

Colour comes from the frameConfirm

All colour on screen comes from what was shot. Type and overlays stay white or black — never coloured.

Type is Archivo, bold capsConfirm

Square corners, no drop shadow, no stroke. If an overlay needs an effect to be readable, it is in the wrong place.

A reel is 9:16Confirm

Reels are shot and cut 9:16 and labelled reels. A 9:16 crop of a carousel is not a reel and reads like one.

06

Do / Don't

The short version. If you read nothing else on this page, read the right-hand column.

Do
  • Find the reference first. Always. The idea is built out of the outlier.
  • Write the text overlays at planning, before anything is shot.
  • Save every reference with a one-line why. Fifteen seconds, every time.
  • Do the maths — views against that account's own median.
  • Steal from outside our category. Better mechanics, no resemblance.
  • Count the cuts in the first five seconds and match that pulse.
  • Check the finished cut back against the reference before it goes out.
Don't
  • Edit first and look for inspiration after. This is the big one. It can never happen.
  • Retrofit a caption or overlay onto a cut that is already locked.
  • Save a post without writing down why it worked.
  • Call a big account's normal post an outlier because the number is large.
  • Copy the subject, the joke or the exact words. Take the mechanism instead.
  • Build on a format from a year ago without checking it still runs.
  • Add colour with type, transitions or overlays.
If you cannot say which outlier an idea came from, it is not an idea yet.
